# Formative learner test plan

## Purpose and participants

Run a small usability and comprehension session with 3–5 health-science students. The aim is to find confusing interactions and teaching language before wider testing, not to measure competence or generate clinical evidence.

Do not collect names, patient information, clinical records, or identifiable health information. Assign neutral participant codes such as P01–P05. Explain that Tete is an educational prototype, participation is voluntary, and the facilitator is evaluating the product rather than the learner.

## Setup

- Use the same production URL and browser profile for the online/offline portion.
- Begin with browser data in a known state and record whether it is reset or pre-populated.
- Have one keyboard-only pass available; permit normal assistive technology where a participant uses it.
- Ask participants to think aloud, but avoid teaching the correct answer during a task.
- Record observable behavior and the learner’s own words. Do not infer a diagnosis, ability, or mastery level.

## Tasks

1. From the home page, find the case library, open a neutral case label, inspect the schematic ECG, and give an interpretation.
2. In Guided Practice, request at least one hint, change an answer, submit, read the structured feedback, and revise once.
3. From feedback and the dashboard, identify a recommended practice area and find its learning resource. Confirm the learner notices that the external link requires internet and the attributed summary remains offline.
4. Explain in their own words what the weighted score means, whether free text is scored, when the prototype rating can change, and why a repeated or guided case is practice-only.
5. After a successful online load, disconnect the device, reload Tete, open a case, use a hint, submit an attempt, and confirm that the progress dashboard retains the attempt after another reload.

Also record:

- viewport/device, input method, browser, and whether offline mode was used;
- where the learner expected controls or information to be;
- wording they could not explain, especially “weighted score,” “expected performance,” “rating eligible,” and “practice guidance”;
- whether focus location, status messages, horizontal ECG scrolling, or zoom caused difficulty;
- whether the learner understood that local progress does not sync and can be lost if browser data is cleared.

## Debrief questions

1. What did you think the next step was at each screen?
2. Which hint helped you reason, and which wording was confusing?
3. What do the score and rating tell you—and what do they not tell you?
4. Did the recommended practice feel relevant? Could you find supporting material?
5. What changed, if anything, when working offline?
6. What is the single most important improvement you would make?

## Analysis and reporting

Summarize task completion counts, recurring confusion, accessibility barriers, and learner suggestions. Keep quotes de-identified. Treat 2 or more participants encountering the same blocker as a priority finding, while still documenting serious one-person accessibility failures. Separate observations from interpretations and proposed changes.

No learner sessions have been run as part of Step 6, and this plan contains no invented results.
